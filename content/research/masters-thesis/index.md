**Novel Model Selection Criterion for Inference of Ising Models**
*M.Sc. Neuroscience, Kavli Institute for Systems Neuroscience — Roudi Group, Norwegian University of Science and Technology (2018–2021)*
Thesis advisors: Dr. Yasser Roudi, Dr. Nicola Bulso

## The question

When you record the activity of a population of neurons, you can ask how its cells are connected — but every answer depends on the model you choose to infer with. Too simple, and you miss real structure; too complex, and you fit noise. **How do you choose the right model?**

## The work

This thesis evaluated a novel information-based Bayesian criterion for network topological inference from spiking neural activity. The work:

- Designed and simulated spiking networks using Ising-model methods, across multiple candidate topologies.
- Compared how well different network structures transferred information — and how inferable they were — via Bayesian model selection.
- Ran large-scale simulation and data collection on NTNU's IDUN high-performance computing cluster (MATLAB).

The result is a principled route to selecting among competing models of neural connectivity — a piece of the broader question of how to read structure out of neural data.
