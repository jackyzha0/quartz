**PhD Candidate in Artificial Intelligence — Oslo Metropolitan University (2022–present)**

## The question

**How does time itself emerge from the plasticity of single neurons?** Biological brains compute with time — spike intervals, synchrony, oscillations — yet artificial neural networks largely treat it as an afterthought, and online temporal learning is virtually absent from deep learning at the edge. My PhD asks whether the brain's time-based mechanisms can become practical learning rules for machines.

## The programme

I design new models of learning for deep spiking neural networks, aimed at neuromorphics and edge hardware:

- **Spike-timing plasticity as a learning signal** — how the timing of individual spikes can shape network weights without back-propagation through time.
- **Oscillator-based computation** — transposing models like striatal beat frequency into reinforcement-learning architectures that find and reproduce periodicity online (see [[research/ner-2025/index|SBF-Automata, IEEE NER 2025]]).
- **Time dynamics from the bottom up** — studying how temporal structure emerges from the plasticity of individual neurons, and what that buys us at the network level.

The goal: machine learning that is **online, always-on, and radically low-energy** — intelligence that belongs on neuromorphic chips and edge devices rather than in datacenters.
